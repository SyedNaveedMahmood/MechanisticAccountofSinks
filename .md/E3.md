# E3 — Circuit Emergence During Pre-Training

**Experiment documentation: training-dynamics harness + run protocol.**
Branch: `E3` (off `E4-with-tqdm`). New module: `emergence_dynamics_analysis.py`.

This document records (1) the scientific design of E3, (2) the code that measures the
attention-sink circuit across pre-training checkpoints, and (3) the exact models, revisions,
commands (PowerShell **and** bash), caveats, and verification steps. It is the reference for
reproducing/auditing the emergence-dynamics runs.

**Target paper:** Ran-Milo, Ofek & Mendel, *A Mechanistic Account of Attention Sinks in GPT-2*
(arXiv:2604.14722). **Venue/theme:** BlackboxNLP 2026 Reproducibility Track — *Generalizability →
dynamics*, addressing the paper's **Limitation §5.2** ("static account of a single trained
checkpoint; how and when the circuit emerges during pre-training is left to future work").

---

## 1. Why E3 (and why it is the novel one)

E1 (cross-scale), E2 (cross-architecture: OPT / GPT-Neo / Qwen2.5), and E4 (residual-sink anatomy)
are complete on `E4-with-tqdm`. All of them analyze **converged** checkpoints. The paper's account
is *static*: it explains the sink on one trained model but says nothing about **when** or **how**
the `b_Q – EPE_1 – W_k` circuit assembles during training. E3 fills exactly that gap and is the
single most novel section available, because it connects the *mechanistic circuit* to its
*emergence trajectory across pre-training over multiple independent seeds* — something neither the
paper nor the broader sink literature has done at this mechanistic resolution.

**Zero training compute.** We reuse Stanford CRFM's public **"Mistral" GPT-2** runs: 5 independent
pre-training seeds per size, each with hundreds of intermediate checkpoints on the HuggingFace Hub,
accessed via `revision="checkpoint-<step>"`. Mistral is **standard GPT-2 architecture** (learned
absolute positional embeddings + a query bias), so *every* validated primitive in this repo applies
unchanged — this is why Mistral, not Pythia/OLMo, is the right vehicle: Pythia (rotary, no query
bias) and OLMo do **not** contain the `b_Q`/`EPE_1` circuit at all, so its emergence cannot be
tracked there.

**Confirmed scope:** GPT-2 **small only**, **5 seeds**, ~18 log-spaced checkpoints, **full
5-analysis suite**.

**Positioning.** Gu et al. (2025, "When Attention Sink Emerges") study *whether/why* the sink
phenomenon appears and its data/optimizer dependence. E3 is finer-grained and orthogonal: it tracks
the *specific* circuit (massive-coordinate identity, `b_Q`–`EPE_1 W_k` alignment, Δ₁ dominance), its
*temporal ordering relative to the behavioral sink*, its *causal-intervention efficacy over
training*, and its *multi-seed coordinate-level universality*.

## 2. Scientific design

### 2.1 Axes (note the change from E4)
- **Pre-training run = error-bar axis.** The **5 Mistral runs are the seed axis** (genuinely
  independent optimizations). This differs from E4, where "seeds" were *data resamples*.
- **Checkpoint step = trajectory axis.**
- **The evaluation data sample is fixed** — one 300-example draw (`--data-seed 0`) from the standard
  GPT-2 BPE (identical across all Mistral runs), reused at *every* run and checkpoint, so
  trajectories are directly comparable and the cross-run band reflects *pre-training* variability,
  not sampling noise.

### 2.2 The six emergence signals (all reuse validated primitives)
For each (run, checkpoint step *t*). Two signals are **pure functions of the weights** (nearly
free); the rest need the fixed forward passes. The pre-softmax score decomposes (paper §3.1) as
`s_{i→j} = T1 (content) + T2 + T3 (Δ_j = b_Q W_kᵀ x_jᵀ, source-agnostic) + T4`, and softmax over
targets cancels the target-constant T2/T4.

| Signal | Meaning | How it is computed (reused primitive) |
|---|---|---|
| **S(t)** sink strength | baseline BOS-attention (second-half → position 0), layers 4–11 | `run_config` baseline + `bos_metric` (from `residual_sink_analysis`) |
| **M(t)** massive activation | `max_d |EPE_1[d]|`, top-3 mean, **#coords > 3σ**, and the coord **indices** | `identify_massive_coords` + the EPE_1 idiom |
| **A(t)** bias alignment (pathway A, Fig 2) | mean over layers 4–11 × heads of `cos(b_Q, W_k·EPE_1)`; + pos-0-vs-rest contrast | `_compute_perhead_epe_cosine` (from `experiments_single_input`) |
| **D(t)** Δ₁ dominance (Fig 1) | softmax attention to position 0 under the **source-agnostic shift T3 alone** | `collect_decomposition_and_alignment` → `attn_delta` |
| **B(t)** query alignment (pathway B, E4) | mean `cos(x_i W_q, W_k·EPE_1)` for second-half sources | `collect_decomposition_and_alignment` → `align_red` |
| **Eff_X(t)** causal efficacy | fraction of the sink removed by X ∈ {Nullify b_Q, Remove-First-PE, Zero-Top-3-W_k} | `run_config` interventions + `bos_metric` |

**Massive coordinates are re-identified per checkpoint** (never hardcoded). We record two index sets:
the full **>3σ** set (variable length — its *count* is signal M's `epe1_n_coords_3sigma`) and a
fixed **top-3** set (`epe1_top3_coords`, the direct analogue of the paper's 138/378/447) used for
the cross-seed universality comparison. **The paper's 138/378/447 are OpenAI-GPT-2-specific; each
Mistral seed lands elsewhere — that difference is the E3.3 result, not a bug.**

### 2.3 Emergence-step extraction & the ordering question
For each (run, signal), min–max normalize the trajectory (converged = last checkpoint) and take
`t_onset` = first step reaching 50% of the converged value (a max-slope step is also reported as a
robustness variant). The headline test: does the circuit **assemble before the behavioral sink**?
We compare `t_onset(A)` (bias alignment) vs `t_onset(S)` (sink) paired across the 5 runs (Wilcoxon +
a plain sign count). Assembly-before-behavior is evidence the circuit *causes* the sink.

### 2.4 The five analyses / deliverables
- **E3.1 Trajectories → `fig_E3A_trajectories.png` + `table_E3_2_converged_vs_paper.csv`.** S/M/A/D
  vs step (log-x), 5-run mean ± band; converged Mistral values next to the paper's OpenAI-GPT-2
  numbers (does the circuit reproduce across independent trainings?).
- **E3.2 Ordering → `fig_E3B_ordering.png` + `table_E3_1_onsets.csv` + `ordering_test.json`.**
  per-signal onset timeline, alignment-vs-sink scatter, paired ordering statistic.
- **E3.3 Universality → `fig_E3C_universality.png` + `final_massive_coords.json`.** cross-seed
  Jaccard of the top-3 coordinate indices + within-seed temporal stability. Either outcome is a
  clean result: same indices ⇒ canonical circuit; different indices, same *function* ⇒ a universal
  mechanism expressed in a seed-arbitrary basis.
- **E3.4 Efficacy → `fig_E3D_efficacy.png`.** intervention efficacy vs step (3 curves) overlaid on
  the median bias-alignment onset — does causal efficacy switch on with the circuit?
- **E3.5 Two-pathway → `fig_E3E_two_pathway.png`.** pathway-A (bias A(t)) vs pathway-B (query B(t))
  alignment onset — which forms first (ties back to E4).

## 3. What the code does (`emergence_dynamics_analysis.py`)

**Design principle: purely additive.** One new module + this doc + one `requirements.txt` line. **No
validated harness is edited.** The module imports the measurement primitives (`run_config`,
`bos_metric`, `collect_decomposition_and_alignment`, `identify_massive_coords`, `compute_ppes`,
`iter_examples`, `_pe_remove_first` from `residual_sink_analysis`; `_compute_perhead_epe_cosine`
from `experiments_single_input`; `compute_band`, `get_initial_embeddings` from
`intervention_analysis`; `sample_benchmark_datasets` from `datasets_loader`).

**The one net-new capability is checkpoint loading at an HF `revision`** — no other harness in the
repo threads it. `load_checkpoint(model_name, device, dtype, revision, cache_dir)` forwards
`revision`/`cache_dir` to `GPT2LMHeadModel.from_pretrained(..., attn_implementation="eager")` (eager
is mandatory to read attention weights) and falls back to the base `gpt2` tokenizer if a checkpoint
repo ships none.

**Runtime checkpoint discovery.** `discover_checkpoints(repo_id)` calls
`huggingface_hub.list_repo_refs` and keeps branches/tags matching `checkpoint-<int>`, so the harness
adapts to whatever schedule Mistral actually published. `select_checkpoints` snaps a set of
log-spaced target steps (dense early, since sinks form early) to the nearest available revisions and
thins to `--n-checkpoints`.

**Streaming download → analyze → purge.** With `--purge-cache`, each checkpoint's cached blobs are
deleted (`huggingface_hub.scan_cache_dir().delete_revisions`) right after it is measured, and the
model is freed (`del`, `gc.collect`, `torch.cuda.empty_cache`), bounding **peak disk to ≈ one
checkpoint (~0.5 GB)** instead of the ~45 GB a full non-streaming download would need.

**Outputs** land under `results/<experiment>/run_<name>/step_XXXXXXXX/metrics.json` (+ per-checkpoint
`run_config.json`), aggregated into `results/<experiment>/aggregate/` (see §7). `--plot-only`
re-aggregates and re-renders from the cached `metrics.json` with no computation.

### CLI (key flags)
```
--mode {trajectories,universality,efficacy,two_pathway,all}   default all
--run-family {gpt2-small,gpt2-medium}                         expands to the 5 Mistral run ids
--runs "<repo,repo,...>"          override the family with explicit HF repo ids
--model-name gpt2                 single-model fidelity mode (one checkpoint, no Mistral sweep)
--revision <rev>                  revision for --model-name (default: main)
--checkpoints {auto|"0,100,..."}  auto = log-spaced targets snapped to available (default)
--n-checkpoints 18                max checkpoints/run under auto
--list-checkpoints                print available revisions per run and exit (Day-1 check)
--data-seed 0                     seed for the FIXED eval sample (kept constant across all runs)
--sample-size 100  --cut-length 40  --layer-mode {scaled,fixed}  --dtype {float32,float16,bfloat16}
--purge-cache   --skip-existing   --plot-only   --smoke-test
```

## 5. Caveats & gotchas

- **Checkpoint availability = the primary risk.** Unverifiable in the dev container. **Day-1 task on
  a networked box:** `--list-checkpoints --run-family gpt2-small`. If a run id is renamed/removed,
  update `MISTRAL_RUNS` in the module (or pass `--runs`). If Mistral is unavailable entirely, the
  **fallbacks** are: (a) any available GPT-2-architecture multi-checkpoint run(s); (b) if only a
  single run exists, keep E3.1/E3.2/E3.4/E3.5 and drop E3.3 (universality needs ≥ 2 runs).
- **Massive-coord indices are seed-specific** — a *result*, re-identified per checkpoint. Do not
  expect 138/378/447 on Mistral.
- **Step-0 = random init is the null control.** Freshly-initialized GPT-2 has **zero attention
  bias**, so pathway A (A(t), Eff_bq) is *identically zero* at init — verified in the smoke test.
  The trajectory grows out of this null; a flat-zero A(t) at the earliest checkpoints is expected,
  not a bug.
- **Disk/IO dominates, not FLOPs.** Use `--purge-cache`; parallelize the 5 runs across the 4 GPUs.
- **fp32 by default** (matches the paper's tight SEs). Drop to fp16 only if VRAM-bound (GPT-2 small
  never is).
- **`attn_implementation="eager"` is required** — the manual-attention path and attention-weight
  extraction depend on it (already set in `load_checkpoint`).

### Suggested GPU allocation (4 PCs)
GPT-2 small is < 1 GB, so any card runs any run in fp32. Split the 5 runs across the 4 PCs (e.g.
5090 takes two, the others one each). Total ≈ a few GPU-hours; **checkpoint download I/O is the
binding cost** — keep `--purge-cache` on.

## 7. Verification

1. **Offline smoke test** (no network, no GPU, ~2 min on CPU):
   ```bash
   python emergence_dynamics_analysis.py --smoke-test --output-dir /tmp/e3_smoke
   ```
   Builds 3 runs × 2 checkpoints of independently-initialized random GPT-2s, runs all five
   analyses, and asserts every signal column is present and finite, `compute_band(12,'scaled')==(3,11)`,
   the T1+T2+T3+T4 decomposition identity holds (`assert_identity`), and all five figures + both
   tables are written. Expected: `SMOKE TEST PASSED`. (A(t) and Eff_bq are exactly 0 at random
   init because b_Q is zero — the correct null.)
2. **Reuse-fidelity check** (§6) on OpenAI `gpt2` reproduces the paper's converged numbers.
3. **Full run** on the GPUs, then `--plot-only` to regenerate all figures/tables from cache.
4. **Ordering robustness:** confirm the `t_onset` ordering is stable under the max-slope onset
   definition (`onsets.csv` reports both) and across the 5 runs.

## 9. Statistical rigor
5 runs → cross-run mean ± band on every trajectory point; onset ordering via paired Wilcoxon + a
sign count (n = 5, so the direction/sign is reported alongside the p-value); cross-seed Jaccard for
coordinate universality; min–max-normalized onsets reported with two definitions (50%-crossing and
max-slope) for robustness.

## 10. Framing for the paper (draft claims E3 supports)
1. "The `b_Q–EPE_1–W_k` sink circuit is not present at initialization; it **assembles over
   pre-training**, and across 5 independent Mistral GPT-2 seeds the circuit signatures (massive
   activations in EPE_1, `b_Q`–`EPE_1 W_k` alignment, Δ₁ dominance) emerge [before / together with]
   the behavioral BOS sink" — resolving the paper's §5.2 open question with a temporal, causal
   account.
2. "The **coordinate indices** carrying the massive activations are [consistent / seed-specific]
   across independent trainings (mean cross-seed Jaccard = …), showing the circuit is a
   [canonical / functionally-universal-but-basis-arbitrary] solution."
3. "The paper's interventions become effective only **after** the alignment forms (efficacy tracks
   A(t) onset), tying the correlational circuit to its causal role over training."
4. "Pathway A (bias) and pathway B (query, the E4 residual) emerge [in sequence / together],
   extending the two-pathway account of E4 into the time domain."

Every claim is publishable at either polarity under the track's negative-results policy.
